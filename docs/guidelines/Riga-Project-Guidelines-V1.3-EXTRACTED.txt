Overview
Welcome to the Riga STEM Project! You’ll be helping us develop a dataset of new, model-
stumping STEM problems across Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Task Description
Each annotation task consists of creating an original, text-only (LaTeX-formatted) STEM
problem which is run through frontier AI models to determine difficulty and contains the
following components:
● Problem: A clear, single, self-contained text-only problem written in LaTeX that
contains a STEM question that can be used to evaluate the reasoning capabilities of
models. The problems must have an open solution path but a closed answer space.
○ Note that you should not be asking multiple independent questions in your
problem and linking them together. A problem is ONE question!
● Domain Classification and Explanation: Your problem must fit into one of the 3 fields
(Physics, Biology, Chemistry) with 15+ domains within each and include a
justification as to why it is the most appropriate selection
● Step-by-Step Solution: A step-by-step worked solution (also written in LaTeX) to the
problem written by experts. The final answer (with units, if applicable) should appear in a
LaTeX boxed environment in the final step
● Answer: The final correct answer that answers the problem
○ Note: if the problem does not specify units, the answer needs to and should
include units!
● Problem Type, Answer Type, and Concepts: Select a description from a list of
possible types that best describes the problem and answer.

Available Domains
You can choose between a Biology, Physics, or Chemistry problem with a corresponding
subdomain on this project. Each problem should be assigned to the Domain and Sub-Domain
that best describe the problem.

These domains should reflect the central subject matter of the problem, not just the notation
used, the final answer format, or techniques that may appear in one possible solution.

See the Domain Description tab for further details

Quality Requirements
To be considered a high-quality submission eligible for Acceptance, the prompt must meet all of
the following:
● Original: All components must be authored by human experts (LLM generated content
is  permitted), and all components within each sample must be original ● Appropriate
STEM Domains: Use STEM concepts in the allowed domains ● Challenging: Stumps
at least one frontier AI model majority of the time.
● Requires reasoning: The problem must require step-by-step reasoning to answer,
rather than reciting memorised facts, theorems, etc. Solutions should be at least 3 steps
● Well-formed: All components must be grammatically or syntactically correct, and without
spelling or LaTeX formatting errors
○ 🚨Currently chemfig is not supported. We plan to add support soon.
○ 🚨mhchem is now supported on the Snorkel platform!
● Unique answer exists: The problem must only have a single unique solution, but it may
have multiple equivalent valid forms
○ “Does not exist” or DNE is not a valid answer on this project
● Clear answer format: The prompt should explicitly state the specified format of the
answer. If the answer is meant to be parametric or expressed in terms of other variables,
that should be clearly stated.
● Reliably verifiable: Answers to the problems and Solution Steps MUST be verifiable
● Self-contained: Aside from the expected STEM knowledge, problems must not contain
references and must not depend on results from a single paper. Any information from
tables or any background from references required to answer the question should be
included in the question itself.

● No shortcutting: The problem must be free of hints or shortcuts that make it “trivial” to
answer
● No guessable answers: The problem must not be easily guessable (e.g. yes/no or

● Problem makes sense: The problem itself must make sense in the context of the

● Objectively Verifiable: Problems should not rely on results from a single paper ● Note:

Answer Requirements
To be considered a high-quality submission eligible for Acceptance, the answer must be
correct.
Additionally, the answer cannot use any notation that hasn't been introduced in the
prompt. Otherwise, this violates the self-contained requirement.
Solution Requirements
To be considered a high-quality submission eligible for Acceptance, the step-by-step solution
must meet the the following requirements:
● Correctness: All components must be valid and arrive at the final answer.
●
Valid Individual Steps: Verify each solution step independently of the answer and
recompute key displayed equalities and numeric value yourself. A correct final answer does
not certify the steps!
●
All Claims are Supported: For every nontrivial claim (especially after 'clearly', 'note that', 'it
follows') confirm the argument is present.
○
For example: Check that cited theorems actually apply, check that the hypotheses
are based on relevant data and sound experimental design, check for physiological
relevance and plausible pathways and mechanisms, check for kinetic and
thermodynamic feasibility, account for all potential intermediate reactions along with
stoichiometric balance, etc.
●
All Assumptions are Explicitly Stated: Every assumption that would change the answer to
the problem if changed must be explicitly stated and justified why it is appropriate in the
context of this problem.
● Non-Computational:
○ The problem must be solved completely without the usage of computer
programming or a computer algebra system.
○ The solution must not contain excessively repetitive operations (e.g. that would be
trivial with computer-assistance) or unreasonably precise computations (e.g., with
many significant figures)
○ Any numerical calculations must be doable with a standard scientific calculator.
● Accurate and Consistent Formatting: All components must be grammatically or
syntactically correct, and without spelling or LaTeX formatting errors. All expressions,
variables, equations, etc. are formatted consistently across all steps of the solution.

Pro Tips for High-Quality Samples
● Ensure you are aligned with the above formatting requirements.
● Double-check your questions are distinct from one another and are not from existing
datasets/competitions.
● Use good grammar, check your spelling, etc.
● Ensure your Solution does not include steps within a step, or any trivial micro-steps.
○ These should be broken up as much as possible, and should only cover the most
significant or substantial steps made to reach the correct solution. You do not
have to include steps that were not important in reaching the solution or points
that aren’t necessary in breaking down these substantial steps.
○ Defer to your best judgement here as to what should/should not be included. You
can refer to the good example steps in the Example Problem tab as a reference
for how these should be written.

LaTeX Help

Having issues with LaTeX formatting? Here are a ton of helpful resources you can utilize:

● Overleaf: offers command completion as well as a side by side source and preview -
a great resource to reference whilst working through formatting issues
● Codecogs: for those unfamiliar with LaTeX syntax, this is an equation editor where you
can click on the symbols you wish to write, and see the LaTeX code in a window, and the
equation formatted in another. Please note that you will need to copy that LaTeX code
and enclose it within $...$ or $$...$$ in your prompt or solution!
● Detextify: for those just beginning to learn LaTeX syntax, here you can hand draw
symbols which are then returned with the LaTeX code for those symbols
● Dainiak: a quick LaTeX style checker
● TeX Studio: integrated writing environment for creating LaTeX documents with
features including syntax-highlighting, integrated viewer, reference checking, etc.

Detailed Submission Instructions
To Start
● In a Riga Submission:
○ You will start by testing your STEM problem against two frontier-level “hard”
models that are intentionally difficult to stump. These models may take a few
minutes to generate a response.
○ Click the blue arrow to run your question through the model and ensure at least
one model consistently gets the wrong answer.
○ Enter your STEM problem in LaTeX in the text box that states: “Enter your prompt
here.”


⚠ PLEASE NOTE: “No Response was generated by the model” is not equivalent to
the model getting an incorrect answer. If you encounter this, please re-run the
models.

Submitting the Question

Once you’re confident you’ve created a problem that consistently stumps one of the
provided models, you’ll complete the rest of the task on the right side of the screen.
● Use the copy and paste your prompt into the text box under the “Problem (Prompt)”
heading on the right-hand side of the UI.

● Select the appropriate domain and sub-domain for the question

● Fill out the Domain Explanation field by providing a brief and clear explanation of why
this domain and subdomain are the best choice for your STEM problem and why it's
more appropriate than the next best domain and subdomain. Justifications should follow
the below structure:

This problem involves <description of the key elements of the problem>,
which are part of <domain and sub domain>.
The problem also involves <description of the additional elements of the problem>,
which are part of <other domain and/or sub domain>.
However, <explanation of why they’re not core to the problem or less advanced>.

Worked Solution
● Provide a step-by-step solution in which each step makes a substantial advance toward
the final answer; avoid breaking the solution into many trivial micro-steps.

The final step of the problem must use LaTeX box format to denote the answer
and end with "Final Answer: $\boxed{<answer goes here>}$"
● Ensure that each step of your solution can be verified by the Reviewer independently of the
answer - recompute key displayed equalities and numeric values yourself prior to submitting.
A correct final answer does not certify the steps!
○ For every nontrivial claim (especially after 'clearly', 'note that', 'it follows') confirm your

○ Every assumption that would change the answer to the problem if changed must be

● Provide all necessary reasoning steps to advance the solution in the “Worked
Solution” field.
○ There should be a minimum of at least 3 steps.
○ Each step must make a significant advancement toward reaching the final answer
rather than simply breaking down the solution into small/trivial pieces.
○ Do not include steps that were not inherently important or points that are not
necessary to understanding how the solution was reached.
○ Ensure each Step is just that, one step. Do not include steps within a step,
substeps, micro-trivial steps, or small/trivial content that isn’t an absolutely
important or necessary step in reaching the final answer.
○ You must enumerate each worked solution step using the following format: Step 1,
Step 2, Step 3, etc
○ All worked solution steps must be explicitly numbered in sequence (Step 1,
Step 2, …)
○ If you are referencing a previous step (i.e., discussing something from Step 2 that
is being recalled or referenced in Step 4, you should explicitly mention that as
“Step 2”)

Overview
As a reviewer, your responsibility is to ensure submitted problems meet the quality standards of
this project. This means checking the following:
1. Is the prompt appropriate for the project?
2. Is the answer correct?
3. Are the solution steps complete, error-free, and well-formatted?
4. Are the labels and categories reasonably accurate?
You are responsible for ensuring the answer to all of these questions is Yes. If it’s not, you must
provide clear, actionable feedback to the original author that helps them improve the
submission. We expect reviews to take 20-30 minutes on average.

Reviewer Responsibility
Each review task involves validating a submitted problem created by an Original Author (OA).
You are not rewriting or re-authoring content. Your job is to ensure the Original Author’s
submission meets ALL high-quality standards, validate accuracy/guideline adherence,
correct minor issues (grammar, formatting, spelling, clarity only), and provide your own
evaluation in the Reviewer Section and leave feedback.
When leaving feedback for the OA, you are both helping them correct the specific submission
and coaching them to improve. Reviewers are expected to follow a strict structure to ensure
feedback is thorough, actionable, and encouraging.
ℹPlease navigate to the Revision or Rejection Notes below for full details on expectations for
feedback.
At the end of the review you will select one of the following options:

Tips for High-Quality Reviews
● Keep your feedback clear, logical, and accurate. Always provide specific, actionable
suggestions!
● Important Checklist:
○ Look for a clear walk-through of the correct answer in the Step-by-Step Solution
■ And ensure these steps are enumerated!
○ Grammar and fluency in both the question and answer
● Emphasize making light edits that help clarify answer correctness but do not
change any of the original content.
● If the OA answer is wrong, your answer should clearly explain why OA is wrong, what
the correct answer is, and why your answer is correct in the “Review Section.”
● If in doubt about a borderline issue, add a note in the “Review Section” field. The
team will review it.
🚨Ask yourself: If the Original Author addressed everything I included in the revision notes,
would it be acceptable upon re-submission? If not, more needs to be added!

For those who have worked on previous Snorkel STEM projects, the review process has

#1. Double-Down on Solution Steps:
The solution steps are often the largest reason for a task not meeting the Acceptance bar, and
being sent back for revision. Moving forward, Reviewers must do the following:

● Verify each step independently of the answer. A correct final answer does not certify
the steps!
● For every nontrivial claim (especially after 'clearly', 'note that', 'it follows') confirm the
argument is present.


#2. Selecting the New “Error Categories”:
To improve the consistency of review feedback, we have implemented new Error Categories in

A one-electron redox couple is described by the electrode reaction
O+e =R,
with standard reduction potential E° for the free species O and R. The solution is ideal and dilute,
so activities are given by concentration divided by the standard concentration c°.
Only the oxidized form binds a ligand L:
O+L=OL,
